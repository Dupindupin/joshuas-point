# Joshua's Point — The House Content Map

**Document type:** Internal editorial planning document  
**Status:** Structure approved; owner source gathering incomplete  
**Public website copy:** No

## Purpose

This document maps the approved structure of `/the-house` to the editorial principles in
`JOSHUA_POINT_HOUSE_SOUL.md`. It is a planning and source-control document, not a draft of the
finished page.

The map exists to keep four things separate:

1. What Joshua's Point has approved as a principle.
2. What the owner has described from lived experience.
3. What still needs factual verification.
4. What is private and must not be published.

Nature remains the central subject throughout. Architecture, people, and daily life should help a
future guest notice the place rather than turn the house into a property brochure.

## Current source status

No detailed owner memories have yet been recorded during the section-by-section editorial process.
The owner's approved wording **“Homy”** and the principles documented in the House Soul are the only
owner-level source currently available for the feeling of the house.

The existing frontend page contains provisional statements about the ridge, slope, Bohol Sea,
morning horizon, shade, heat, rooflines, timber, and stone. These statements are not approved facts
for the redesigned page. They must not be carried forward without owner and, where appropriate,
architectural or geographical verification.

## Publication boundary

The following remain excluded from every section unless the owner later gives explicit, specific
permission:

- Private details about Tobias and his wife.
- The private origin or meaning of the Joshua's Point name.
- Family memories, identities, conversations, or personal circumstances.
- Guest stories or identifiable images without consent.
- Internal source notes that have not been approved as public material.

## 1. Editorial Hero

### Emotional purpose

Create the feeling of encountering the house within its setting. The first impression should direct
attention outward—to land, weather, light, sea, and mountains—before asking the visitor to consider
the building.

### Core message

The house belongs in a relationship with the landscape. It exists to help people notice and live
with that landscape, not to compete with it.

This is a message brief, not proposed public wording.

### Approved facts

- Nature is the hero.
- The house frames the landscape rather than competing with it.
- Sea and mountains belong to the identity of Joshua's Point.
- The public experience should feel calm, warm, welcoming, and unpressured.
- The opening should be photography-first.

No exact view, orientation, landform, sea, island, mountain, or architectural response is yet an
approved fact.

### Private information that must remain excluded

- The private emotional origin of Joshua's Point.
- The private origin of the name.
- Details about the owners' relationship or first visit that have not been approved for publication.
- Any family image or memory used to manufacture an emotional opening.

### Required photography

- One defining panoramic or wide landscape image.
- The house should appear as part of the setting rather than as an isolated façade.
- Prefer a human-height viewpoint and natural conditions.
- Preserve negative space for calm composition; do not rely on a text-heavy overlay.
- Capture a mobile-safe crop or record an approved focal position.
- Supply accurate alt text and, only when helpful, a concise factual caption.

### Missing owner input

- What someone actually sees and senses on first encountering the house.
- Which part of the land or view first holds attention.
- A public-safe description of the relationship between land and house.
- What the owner hopes a guest feels on arrival, separated from what guests have actually said.
- Whether the photographed view is representative across ordinary weather and seasons.

## 3. Shared Heart of the House

### Emotional purpose

Show the house as a place where people cook, eat, talk, rest, and share time while remaining
connected to nature. The atmosphere should be social without suggesting parties, events, or staged
hospitality.

### Core message

The living room, kitchen, dining area, and deck belong to one shared rhythm. Their importance comes
from how people use them together, not from their dimensions or finishes.

### Approved facts

- Connected living spaces are an approved architectural and editorial principle.
- The living room, kitchen, and dining area belong to a shared rhythm.
- The house is intended to support time with friends and family.
- Joshua's Point is not a party place.
- Real moments are preferred to staged models or artificial social scenes.

No specific gathering, meal, room arrangement, or daily use pattern has yet been recorded as owner
source.

### Private information that must remain excluded

- Family identities, conversations, celebrations, or personal circumstances.
- A private gathering presented publicly without the approval of everyone identifiable.
- Guest memories or photographs without consent.
- Claims that particular activities always happen in the space.

### Required photography

- One primary landscape image showing the relationship among living, kitchen, dining, deck, and the
  outside, as far as one honest viewpoint allows.
- An optional secondary detail showing real use rather than decoration.
- If people appear, use a small, natural group with explicit consent.
- Avoid a wide-angle image designed chiefly to make the room look larger.
- Record accurate captions and alt text.

### Missing owner input

- The actual spatial relationship among the shared rooms and deck.
- Where people naturally gather and at what times.
- How cooking and eating usually unfold.
- One shareable, owner-approved memory that expresses the space without exposing private family
  information.
- What someone can see, hear, or feel from these rooms when the doors are open or closed.

## 5. Between Inside and Outside

providing shelter, and the pool belonging to the wider relationship between architecture and
landscape.

### Core message

Indoor/outdoor living is a set of real thresholds and routines, not a design slogan. Weather and
daily use should explain the architecture.

### Approved facts

- The deck is a central threshold in the editorial identity of Joshua's Point.
- Open sliding doors, connected living spaces, roof, and pool contribute to the indoor/outdoor
  relationship.
- The pool should not be treated as an isolated amenity.
- Architecture should frame nature rather than compete with it.

No roof performance, shading strategy, ventilation effect, drainage behavior, pool alignment,
material, dimension, or design intent has yet been verified.

### Private information that must remain excluded

- Family or guest images without consent.
- The emotional origin of a design decision when it belongs to the private family story.
- Security-sensitive information about doors, access, or property operation.

### Required photography

- One portrait or vertical image of an open threshold.
- One detail of the deck, roof edge, shade, or weather transition.
- One wider landscape image showing the house–deck–pool–landscape relationship.
- Photograph both openness and shelter honestly.
- Avoid presenting the pool as a freestanding feature shot.
- Provide an alt description and a concise, verified caption for each meaningful image.
