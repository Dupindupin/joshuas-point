# Joshua's Point — The House Image Shoot List

**Document type:** Internal field photography checklist
**Route supported:** `/the-house`
**Status:** Pre-shoot planning; all viewpoints and activities require on-site and owner confirmation
**Public website copy:** No

## Purpose

This checklist defines the practical photography still needed to complete the House page before
Sanity integration. It is based on `THE_HOUSE_PHOTOGRAPHY_BRIEF.md`,
`THE_HOUSE_SELECTED_PHOTOGRAPHY.md`, and `THE_HOUSE_VERIFICATION_RECORD.md`.

Suggested camera positions are directions to investigate, not statements about the house. The
photographer and owner must confirm that each position truthfully shows the intended relationship.
Do not manufacture a routine, weather condition, architectural relationship, or human moment for
the sake of completing the list.

## 1. Missing essential images

## The View

**Current status:** Missing

### Editorial purpose

Create the page's principal outward visual pause. This photograph should let the landscape stand on
its own from a real, regularly experienced position at or immediately around the house. It must not
repeat the Hero's house-within-landscape role.

### Suggested camera position

- Begin at a normal standing or seated position from which the owner genuinely experiences the
  outward view.
- Test positions within a shared room, at an open threshold, or on the deck only after the owner
  identifies the representative viewpoint.
- Keep foreground architecture only when it helps locate the viewer without taking over the image.
- Record the precise internal camera position and direction for verification; review separately how
  much location detail may be public.

### Feeling

- Peaceful
- Expansive without spectacle
- Connected to nature
- Patient enough to hold the page with little or no caption

### What to avoid

- Reusing the current Hero candidate.
- An isolated scenic photograph taken from a position guests do not normally occupy.
- Telephoto compression, extreme panorama stitching, tilted horizon, or exaggerated scale.
- Replaced skies, excessive saturation, artificial haze removal, or erased weather.
- Naming water, islands, mountains, ridges, towns, directions, sunrise, or sunset before
  verification.

### Required capture notes

- Viewpoint, direction, date, approximate time, and weather.
- Candidate landmarks for later verification.
- Lens and any panorama method.
- Wide desktop crop and narrower mobile-safe crop.

## Rain

**Current status:** Missing

### Editorial purpose

Show rain as part of living at Joshua's Point through the relationship among shelter, architecture,
and outside conditions. The image should be honest about weather rather than romanticizing or
hiding it.

### Suggested camera position

- Use a safe, normally occupied position beneath shelter or immediately inside an opening.
- Look for a frame that includes both the sheltered edge of the house and visible rain outside.
- Consider the deck, roof edge, pool surface, vegetation, or distant landscape only where they make
  rainfall legible naturally.
- Confirm with the owner how the photographed place is actually used during rain.

### Feeling

- Sheltered
- Peaceful
- Close to weather
- Homy without becoming sentimental

### What to avoid

- Artificial rain or digitally added rain.
- Unsafe camera placement or activity during severe weather.
- Darkening the image solely to create drama.
- Staged rainy-day rituals.
- Claims about drainage, access, power, safety, or seasonal performance based on one event.

### Required capture notes

- Date, time, viewpoint, and observable weather conditions.
- Where the camera was sheltered.
- Any practical effects visible in the frame.
- Whether the event is ordinary enough to represent publicly.

## Wider landscape Hero

**Current status:** Missing as the definitive Hero. A narrower visual candidate exists.

### Editorial purpose

Introduce the house within its wider land and natural setting. The photograph should show that the
building belongs to a relationship with the landscape rather than present it as an isolated object.

### Suggested camera position

- Walk the ordinary arrival and circulation areas with the owner before choosing the frame.
- Look for a human-height position that includes meaningful land or vegetation around the house.
- Test a moderate distance that reveals the house's place in the setting without exposing
  security-sensitive access information.
- Capture the position in representative natural conditions rather than waiting only for dramatic
  weather.

### Feeling

- Quiet arrival
- Grounded
- Connected to nature
- Inhabitable rather than monumental

### What to avoid

- A façade-only property photograph.
- A pool-led resort composition.
- Wide-angle distortion used to make the house appear larger.
- Aerial spectacle as the only view of the setting.
- Foreground furniture dominating the composition.
- Communications infrastructure or unrelated visual clutter becoming a primary focal point unless
  it cannot be framed honestly otherwise.
- Unverified claims about ridge, slope, sea, mountains, orientation, or design response.

### Required capture notes

- Camera position, focal length, date, time, and weather.
- Visible geography for later verification.
- Landscape context retained or excluded by the frame.
- Responsive focal position and mobile crop.

## Morning

**Current status:** Missing

The current Hero development asset is reserved for the Hero role. It cannot also serve as Morning,
so a dedicated photograph is still required.

### Editorial purpose

Record how a real morning begins through light, weather, an owner-confirmed place, or an ordinary
routine. Morning should be observed rather than styled.

### Suggested camera position

- Ask the owner where morning is first noticed or normally spent.
- Photograph from that real position at the relevant time.
- Include an interior edge, threshold, deck, or landscape only when it belongs to the observed
  morning.
- Capture alternative orientations if the moment allows, without changing the routine for the
  camera.

### Feeling

- Quietly awake
- Homy
- Unhurried
- Attentive to changing light

### What to avoid

- Generic coffee, breakfast, fruit, or bed styling.
- Calling the image sunrise or first light without directional and time verification.
- Over-warm processing or artificial window light.
- Presenting a private family ritual as a typical guest experience.
- Reusing the final Hero image in this section.

### Required capture notes

- Exact date, approximate time, weather, room or outdoor position, and light direction.
- The owner observation that gives the moment editorial relevance.
- Whether visible objects or activity were already present or arranged.

## Material details

**Current status:** Needs verification before further shooting

The current file
`61919986-5595-4830-A7A8-99AF1F658A4B_1_105_c.jpeg` may support a timber or joinery story, but no
material identity or design relevance is verified.

### Editorial purpose

Make one or more confirmed parts of the house tactile through use, light, weathering, construction,
or care. Material photographs should support lived architecture rather than resemble a product
catalogue.

### Suggested camera position

- Verify the defining materials and details with the owner, architect, or project records first.
- Photograph each approved subject close enough to show texture while retaining enough context to
  locate it within the house.
- Potential subjects should arise from verification, not from a required checklist.
- Capture a wider reference frame for internal identification even when the published image is a
  close detail.

### Feeling

- Tactile
- Warm
- Honest about use and aging
- Connected to light and weather

### What to avoid

- Assuming timber species, stone type, origin, craft method, sustainability, or performance.
- Photographing generic decoration or appliances as architectural detail.
- Concealing ordinary wear to make finishes appear untouched.
- Abstract close-ups that lose all relationship with the house.
- Treating Light or Air as literal materials without an approved editorial decision.

### Required capture notes

- Verified material name, application, exact location, source, and current condition.
- Designer, architect, craftsperson, or supplier credit only where confirmed and approved.
- Any cleaning, moving, styling, or retouching undertaken for the image.

## Human moments — optional

**Current status:** Missing; not required for page completion

### Editorial purpose

Introduce real human scale or shared life only if it adds something that architecture, objects, and
ordinary traces of use cannot communicate alone.

### Suggested camera position

- Observe a genuine, approved activity from a respectful distance.
- Keep house and nature present so the person does not become a model or advertisement.
- Consider non-identifying gestures, hands, backs, or distant figures, while recognizing that
  clothing, context, and body shape may still identify someone.
- Agree the moment and privacy boundary before taking photographs.

### Feeling

- Homy
- Natural
- Quietly shared
- Unperformed

### What to avoid

- Staged models or invented guest experiences.
- Private family memories used as public emotional proof.
- Event-scale groups, party imagery, or aspirational posing.
- Publishing guests, family, neighbours, staff, or contractors through assumed consent.
- Images involving minors without a specific need, guardian consent, and safeguarding review.

### Required capture notes

- Identity and contact details stored securely outside public editorial content.
- Written consent scope, media, territory, duration, and withdrawal process.
- Whether the activity is ordinary and public-safe.
- Caption and alt-text privacy limits.

## 2. Technical requirements

## Original resolution and delivery

- Deliver source-original files, not messaging-app, social-media, or device-preview derivatives.
- Preserve original pixel dimensions, color profile, and metadata in the controlled source archive.
- Do not upscale the current `1086 × 724`, `724 × 1086`, or `540 × 360` files as a substitute for
  recovering originals.
- For new capture, use the camera's full native resolution and highest practical quality setting.
- Retain RAW files when available, together with non-destructive editing records and a high-quality
  master export.
- Do not set a false universal minimum before the final crop is known. Confirm that the approved
  crop has sufficient native pixels for its maximum rendered size and high-density displays.
- Use stable asset identifiers and preserve capture-to-export version history.

## Orientation requirements

| Editorial role           | Preferred capture                                                                 |
| ------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Hero                     | Wide landscape or panoramic, with additional crop allowance.                      |
| The View                 | Panoramic landscape plus a safe narrower crop.                                    |
| Shared Heart             | Landscape; optional secondary detail only if editorially distinct.                |
| Indoor/Outdoor threshold | Capture both portrait and landscape where the real relationship permits.          |
| Deck and roof            | Portrait or contextual detail, plus a wider verification frame.                   |
| Pool relationship        | Landscape.                                                                        |
| Morning                  | Orientation follows the confirmed moment; capture an alternative where possible.  |
| Rain                     | Orientation follows visibility of rain and shelter; do not compromise safety.     |
| Evening                  | Landscape or portrait according to the real interior/exterior relationship.       |
| Materials                | Portrait, landscape detail, and a wider identification frame as needed.           |
| Human moments            | Orientation follows the genuine activity; identity-safe alternatives recommended. |

Orientation is editorial guidance, not a demand to force a scene into the current layout. Controlled
components may omit an image or adapt within their supported ratios if the truthful photograph
requires it.

## Mobile-safe composition

- Capture enough space around the principal subject for narrower crops without losing the
  relationship being documented.
- Record the intended focal point for every candidate.
- Keep important people, architectural edges, landmarks, and gestures away from unsafe crop zones.
- Do not place essential meaning at both extreme sides of a wide frame if a mobile crop cannot hold
  it.
- Review each final crop on a narrow phone, large phone, tablet portrait, desktop, and wide desktop.
- Write alt text from the final rendered crop, not from the uncropped original.
- Do not preserve an unsuitable photograph merely because it fits a desktop placeholder.

## Rights, privacy, and consent

- Record photographer, copyright owner, license or assignment, credit format, permitted media,
  territory, duration, exclusivity, and modification rights.
- Confirm permission to create crops, responsive derivatives, color corrections, and optimized web
  formats.
- Disclose compositing, object removal, sky replacement, and generative alteration. Misleading
  documentary edits are not acceptable.
- Obtain explicit, recorded consent for every identifiable person. Do not assume consent based on
  relationship to the property.
- Apply additional safeguarding review to any image involving a minor; omit the image when the need
  is unclear.
- Inspect high-resolution frames for keys, access controls, personal documents, screens, vehicle
  details, artwork, branding, private objects, and other sensitive information.
- Remove sensitive coordinates and device metadata from public derivatives while retaining needed
  source records securely.
- Keep releases, private identities, owner memories, and sensitive verification material outside
  public website and Sanity content fields.

### Integration gate

Photography is ready for Sanity modelling when:

- The eight-image required set has real, approved assets or an intentionally documented omission.
- Hero and The View have distinct editorial jobs.
- Every retained daily moment has owner source; incomplete moments are omitted.
- Materials render only when a verified material story exists.
- Source originals, rights, credits, crops, focal positions, captions, alt text, privacy, and consent
  decisions are complete.
- The complete sequence has passed desktop and mobile review for repetition, pace, crop quality,
  page weight, and the three protected feelings: Homy, Connected to nature, and Peaceful.
