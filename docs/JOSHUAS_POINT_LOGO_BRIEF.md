# Joshua's Point — Logo Brief

**Document type:** Internal brand identity brief
**Status:** Creative direction before logo design
**Purpose:** Guide future logo exploration without prescribing or creating a finished mark

## Central idea

The Joshua's Point identity should feel like a quiet sign of place: warm, recognisable, and closely
connected to the landscape. It should support photography and storytelling rather than compete with
them.

The logo must express Joshua's Point as Tobias sharing a place he genuinely knows and loves. It
must not present Joshua's Point as a hotel, resort, or status brand.

## 1. Brand essence

Joshua's Point is:

- **Quiet:** composed, spacious, and confident without demanding attention.
- **Warm:** personal and welcoming, with the natural character of timber, evening light, and a
  lived-in home.
- **Curious:** an invitation to notice the landscape and discover Southern Negros slowly.
- **Adventurous:** open to journeys, weather, water, mountains, and unfamiliar places, without
  becoming an outdoor-sports identity.
- **Humble:** honest, restrained, and free from prestige signals.
- **Nature-connected:** rooted in the relationship between the house, land, sea, mountains, light,
  and changing weather.

The identity should feel timeless rather than nostalgic, and architectural without becoming cold or
formal.

### Desired first impression

The mark should suggest that Joshua's Point is a meaningful place worth approaching slowly. It
should leave room for curiosity rather than explaining every aspect of the place at once.

### Role of the identity

The identity is a frame, not the hero. Nature, owner photography, and Tobias's observations remain
the most expressive parts of Joshua's Point.

## 2. Visual themes to explore

These themes are starting points for design exploration. They should be interpreted abstractly and
with restraint; the final identity does not need to combine all of them.

### Sea horizon

A calm horizontal gesture may express the Bohol Sea, distance, stillness, and the way the view opens
from the house. It should feel specific to looking outward, not like a generic wave or beach symbol.

### Mountain silhouette

A restrained contour may reference the relationship with Mount Talinis and the land behind the
house. Avoid detailed scenic illustration, outdoor-brand peaks, or dramatic summit imagery.

### Point and location

The idea of a point may be explored as a meeting place, a position in the landscape, or a quiet
moment of orientation. Avoid a conventional map pin unless it is transformed into something
distinctly Joshua's Point.

### Landscape connection

Consider the meeting of horizontal and rising forms: sea and mountain, distance and shelter, or
land and sky. Negative space may communicate these relationships more effectively than literal
illustration.

### House and nature relationship

Architectural lines may frame rather than depict the landscape. Possible references include an
opening, threshold, roof edge, covered deck, or the movement between inside and outside. The house
should never appear as a property icon or real-estate outline.

### Wooden warmth

Warmth may come through proportion, line character, material application, or restrained color. It
should evoke natural timber and touch without relying on rustic textures, woodgrain effects, or
craft-brand clichés.

### Productive creative tensions

Strong directions may explore the balance between:

- Horizontal calm and gentle movement.
- Architectural precision and natural irregularity.
- Shelter and openness.
- Home and exploration.
- Sea and mountain.

## 3. What to avoid

The identity must avoid:

- Generic resort, villa, accommodation, or real-estate symbols.
- Palm trees, sunsets, cocktails, huts, shells, and tropical-island clichés.
- Generic waves, mountain badges, compass roses, and unmodified map pins.
- Crests, monograms, seals, or metallic effects that imply luxury-hotel status.
- Excessively polished, corporate, or technology-led geometry.
- Adventure-brand aggression, sharp summit motifs, or expedition styling.
- Rustic nostalgia, distressed textures, and ornamental heritage styling.
- Multiple icons attempting to explain the sea, house, mountains, and travel at once.
- Fine detail that disappears at favicon or mobile-header size.
- Trend-led lettering that will date quickly.
- A symbol that overwhelms the Joshua's Point name or owner photography.

The apostrophe in **Joshua's Point** is part of the name and must remain correct and legible in every
wordmark application.

## 4. Required future assets

No assets are created as part of this brief. A future identity phase should deliver the following as
one coherent system.

### Primary logo

- Full Joshua's Point wordmark, with an optional restrained symbol if the concept benefits from one.
- Suitable for wide header, footer, editorial cover, and formal brand applications.
- Must remain clear at common website-header sizes.

### Compact mark

- A simplified mark for constrained spaces.
- Must work without forcing the full identity into a decorative badge.
- Should remain recognisable independently while clearly belonging to the primary logo.

### Favicon

- Purpose-built for very small sizes rather than a scaled-down primary logo.
- Required checks at 16, 32, 48, 180, and 512 pixels.
- Must remain distinguishable in Light and Dark browser interfaces.

### Social profile image

- Square composition with generous safe space for circular platform crops.
- Legible at small feed and comment sizes.
- Should not depend on photography or fine wordmark text to remain recognisable.

### Light version

- Designed for warm linen, cream, sand, and light photographic contexts.
- Uses the approved charcoal, forest, timber, or restrained ocean character as appropriate.

### Dark version

- Designed for deep charcoal, warm stone, muted forest, and deep ocean contexts.
- Uses warm off-white and restrained warmth; it must not be an automatic color inversion.

### Delivery expectations

Future approved assets should include:

- Vector masters in SVG.
- Print-ready vector or PDF artwork where required.
- Optimised web exports.
- Single-color versions.
- Clear-space and minimum-size guidance.
- Accessible naming and a documented fallback wordmark.
- Color specifications mapped to the existing semantic design tokens.

## 5. Usage contexts

### Header

The identity should sit quietly above photography and editorial content. It must work over both
transparent hero imagery and solid Light or Dark header surfaces without requiring a large backing
shape.

### Footer

The footer application should feel like the signature on the final page of a magazine. The logo
may be slightly more present than in the header but should remain calm and spacious.

### Social platforms

The compact mark or social profile image should remain identifiable inside circular and square
crops. It should coexist with owner photography without making social posts feel branded or
promotional.

### Open Graph sharing

The logo should support restrained placement on 1200 × 630 social images. Photography and the page
story remain primary; the mark provides authorship rather than an advertisement.

### PDF and book editions

The primary logo should work on covers, title pages, spines, and closing pages for future Joshua's
Point guides. It must reproduce clearly in color and single-color print.

### Future Explorer platform

The compact mark may identify Joshua's Point as the origin of the Explorer experience. It should
not resemble a map-provider icon or compete with map markers, labels, and editorial navigation.

## Relationship to the existing design system

- **Display typeface:** Newsreader.
- **Body and interface typeface:** Manrope.
- **Color character:** warm linen, sand, stone, charcoal, forest, timber, restrained ocean, and warm
  evening tones.
- **Photography:** truthful, natural, and more visually expressive than the interface.
- **Motion:** subtle, calm, and never necessary for recognition.

Logo exploration may consider Newsreader as a reference for the wordmark's editorial character,
but it should test whether an unmodified typeset name is sufficiently distinctive. Any custom
lettering must remain compatible with the established typography rather than introducing a third
visual voice.

## Evaluation criteria

Each future concept should be reviewed against these questions:

1. Does it feel like a place and a home rather than a hotel?
2. Does it express quiet warmth without becoming passive or generic?
3. Is the relationship between landscape, shelter, and discovery present without literal clutter?
4. Does it remain humble beside strong photography?
5. Is the Joshua's Point name—including its apostrophe—clear and memorable?
6. Does it work at favicon, mobile-header, social-profile, and printed-cover sizes?
7. Does it work naturally in the approved Light and Dark atmospheres?
8. Can it be reproduced in one color without losing its idea?
9. Does it avoid tropical, resort, luxury, real-estate, and outdoor-brand conventions?
10. Could it still feel appropriate in ten years?

## Direction for the future design phase

The first logo exploration should compare a small number of genuinely different approaches rather
than many decorative variations:

1. A wordmark-led identity with very restrained custom detail.
2. A landscape relationship expressed through negative space or a minimal line.
3. An architectural threshold or point that frames, rather than illustrates, nature.

Concepts should first be evaluated in single color and at small size. Color, texture, animation,
and photographic applications should come only after the underlying form is clear.

The successful identity will not try to tell the entire Joshua's Point story. It will create a
quiet, recognisable place for that story to begin.
