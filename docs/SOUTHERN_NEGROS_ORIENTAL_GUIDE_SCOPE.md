# Joshua's Point — Southern Negros Oriental Guide Scope

## Status and purpose

This document defines the editorial scope of a first Southern Negros Oriental guide created from
the existing [Ultimate Negros Travel Guide](https://guide.joshuaspoint.com/). It is a planning and
classification document only. It does not migrate content, approve legacy claims, change the
frontend, or modify Sanity.

The source guide was reviewed on **11 August 2026** through its:

- [Guide home](https://guide.joshuaspoint.com/)
- [Destination archive](https://guide.joshuaspoint.com/destinations/)
- [Explorer map](https://guide.joshuaspoint.com/explore-map/)
- [Digital-edition preview](https://guide.joshuaspoint.com/product/ultimate-negros-travel-guide-digital/)

The destination archive contains 105 entries. Every entry is classified in Section 7.

## Guiding question

> What should guests discover while staying at Joshua's Point?

Every inclusion must answer that question. The guide begins and ends at Joshua's Point in Calango,
Zamboanguita. It is not an encyclopedia of Negros and not a directory of every tourism business.

The guide should help a guest understand:

- What belongs naturally in a day from Joshua's Point.
- Why Tobias recommends a place or journey.
- How coast, mountain, forest, lake, waterfall, and diving relate to one another.
- What must be prepared or confirmed before leaving.
- When the journey itself deserves as much attention as the destination.

## 1. Geographic scope

### Included core

- **Joshua's Point and Calango, Zamboanguita** — the emotional and geographic center.
- **Zamboanguita** — local orientation, Malatapay, the coast, and the Apo Island gateway.
- **Dauin** — coast, community, marine sanctuaries, diving, food, and useful guest services.
- **Valencia** — waterfalls, forest, geothermal highlands, and mountain journeys.
- **Siaton** — Lake Balanan, Tambobo Bay, coastal and mountain landscapes.
- **Apo Island** — island, conservation, snorkeling and diving context, reached through the
  southern coast.
- **Dumaguete gateway** — airport, port, city orientation, supplies, food, and the transition south.
- **Southern Negros Oriental routes** — owner-approved roads and journeys connecting these places.

### Excluded from this edition

- Bacolod and the Bacolod–Silay gateway.
- Sipalay, Cauayan, Hinoba-an, and the western coast.
- Negros Occidental destinations and services.
- Northern Negros destinations.
- Siquijor destinations, services, and island loops.
- Mabinay, Bais, Manjuyod, and other places outside the first-edition southern radius.

Exclusion is a product-scope decision, not a judgment about quality. Strong material may be retained
in the source archive for a later western, northern, or Siquijor edition.

### Borderline places

Balinsasayao Twin Lakes Natural Park is relevant to many Dumaguete-based journeys but sits outside
the tight first-edition southern core. It is classified as **exclude from Version 1 / retain for a
future Negros Oriental extension** unless Tobias explicitly wants it as a longer day from Joshua's
Point.

## 2. Editorial center

Joshua's Point must remain more than the starting coordinate. It provides the point of view:

- Morning begins at the house, with the day's weather and landscape visible.
- Recommendations come from Tobias's knowledge and experience.
- Routes are described as days that leave and return to Joshua's Point.
- Practical advice is selected for guests staying in Calango, not for an abstract traveler.
- The guide favors a few meaningful discoveries over completion and volume.

The tone remains personal, grounded, observant, and quietly enthusiastic. It should sound like
Tobias showing a guest where he would go—not a regional tourism board or hotel concierge.

## 3. Edition promise

### What this edition should provide

- A clear sense of Southern Negros Oriental from one trusted base.
- A deliberately selected set of places rather than the full legacy directory.
- Owner-led destination and route narratives.
- Practical gateway and preparation information.
- An editorial diving overview with technical boundaries.
- Verified maps and accessible text alternatives.
- Offline-friendly organization suitable for a later PDF or book.

### What it should not become

- A list of competing hotels and resorts.
- An unmaintainable directory of every café, ATM, pharmacy, or transport provider.
- A technical dive manual.
- A collection of generic descriptions detached from owner experience.
- A route planner that promises current road, weather, sea, or access conditions.
- A repackaging of western Negros or Siquijor content under a southern title.

## 5. Classification definitions

### Migrate

The entry belongs in the edition as a canonical destination, area, or story. Preserve strong source
material, improve it carefully, and map it to the existing Joshua's Point document architecture.

### Rewrite

The subject belongs, but the current entry relies too heavily on generic description, changing
operational details, technical claims, or a framing that does not yet sound like Tobias. Rebuild it
from the legacy source plus approved owner knowledge; do not rewrite from zero unnecessarily.

### Merge

The information is useful, but it should not remain a standalone destination. Fold it into a
gateway, locality, food, supplies, transport, or practical field-notes section. Keep source and
review dates.

### Exclude

The entry is outside the geographic/product scope, duplicates another record, promotes alternative
accommodation without a clear guide role, or creates more maintenance burden than guest value.
Retain it in the legacy archive for possible future editions.

## 8. Content gaps revealed by the classification

### Zamboanguita needs an owner-led center

The legacy archive contains Malatapay Market but no complete Zamboanguita or Calango editorial
entry. The first edition therefore needs approved source material about:

- How Tobias introduces Zamboanguita to guests.
- The character of the coast and local roads around Joshua's Point.
- What belongs in an unhurried nearby morning or afternoon.
- The relationship between Calango, Malatapay, Dauin, Siaton, and Apo Island.

This should not be filled with generic municipal copy.

### Routes need editorial assembly

The legacy guide contains route fragments and gateway advice rather than a dedicated scenic-route
archive. The new platform's owner-approved route documents should lead. Legacy access passages can
be merged only where they add genuine practical or observational value.

### Diving needs a clear boundary

The edition should distinguish:

- **Guest discovery:** why an area matters, its relationship to coast and community, photography,
  and responsible behavior.
- **Technical guidance:** depth, current, visibility, conditions, level, access, and safety, which
  require qualified review.

Missing underwater photography does not block the edition plan.

### Commercial recommendations need owner intent

The legacy guide contains many businesses. Before merging any food, transport, rental, or service
entry, record whether it is:

- A current Tobias recommendation.
- A useful but neutral practical listing.
- No longer necessary.

The guide should never imply endorsement merely because a record existed in the old directory.

## 9. Migration order for this edition

1. **Lock the scope and classification.** Resolve only the owner decisions in Section 10.
2. **Migrate core canonical places.** Apo Island, Casaroro Falls, Lake Balanan, Dauin, Valencia,
   Siaton, Malatapay Market, Pulangbato Falls, Tambobo Bay, and Dauin Marine Sanctuaries.
3. **Create the owner-led Zamboanguita source record.** Keep Joshua's Point central.
4. **Assemble the Dumaguete gateway.** Merge airport, port, city, and selected practical records.
5. **Refine specialist stories.** Baslay Hot Spring, Forest Camp, and Mount Talinis.
6. **Connect owner-approved scenic routes.** Reuse existing route paths, stops, and relationships.
7. **Review diving content.** Publish editorial guidance first; block unsupported technical fields.
8. **Curate practical notes.** Include only services that genuinely help Joshua's Point guests.
9. **Build the premium edition structure.** Reference canonical documents instead of duplicating
   facts.

## 10. Owner decisions needed before migration

1. Should Balinsasayao Twin Lakes be included as a longer day from Joshua's Point, or saved for a
   later Negros Oriental extension?
2. Which legacy Dauin and Dumaguete food entries are current Tobias recommendations?
3. Should any scooter rental or local transport provider be named, or should guidance remain
   provider-neutral?
4. Does Kookoo's Nest have a meaningful owner-recommended place in the Siaton story despite being
   alternative accommodation, or should it remain excluded?
5. Is Forest Camp personally recommended, and what is Tobias's first-hand reason for including it?
6. Who will provide qualified review for technical Dauin and Apo Island dive information?
7. What public-safe observations should define Zamboanguita and Calango beyond Malatapay Market?
8. Should the edition title remain **Southern Negros Oriental Guide**, or use a more personal title
   such as **Southern Negros Oriental from Joshua's Point**?

## Final scope statement

This edition is not the old Ultimate Negros directory with fewer entries. It is a newly focused
edit of existing material, arranged around the lived geography of Joshua's Point.

Its center is the house. Its immediate world is Zamboanguita, Dauin, Valencia, Siaton, Apo Island,
and the Dumaguete gateway. Its subject is the relationship between sea, mountain, road, nature,
diving, and the quiet return home.
