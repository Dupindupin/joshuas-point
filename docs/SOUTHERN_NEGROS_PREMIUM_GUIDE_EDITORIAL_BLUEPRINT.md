# Joshua's Point — Southern Negros Premium Guide Edition 1 Editorial Blueprint

## Status and purpose

This document is the editorial production blueprint for Edition 1 of the proposed Southern Negros
premium guide. It uses:

- `SOUTHERN_NEGROS_ORIENTAL_GUIDE_SCOPE.md`
- `SOUTHERN_NEGROS_PREMIUM_GUIDE_PRODUCT_DEFINITION.md`

It defines the publication structure, chapter responsibilities, premium journey format, writing
standard, visual requirements, and production workflow. It does not create final copy, migrate
content, build the frontend, or modify Sanity.

The working title remains **Southern Negros Oriental from Joshua's Point** until Tobias approves the
final product name.

## Editorial premise

The guide begins with one question:

> What should guests discover while staying at Joshua's Point?

Joshua's Point is the center of the publication, not simply a pin on the map. Each chapter should
help a reader leave the house with purpose, experience one part of Southern Negros Oriental with
care, and return without feeling that the day was a list to complete.

The premium editorial unit is the **journey**. Destination profiles support the journey; they do not
replace it.

### Three-layer editorial model

Every premium journey combines three distinct forms of knowledge:

1. **The place itself** — verified general context drawn from reliable external sources and useful
   existing Joshua's Point content.
2. **Tobias's personal experience** — observations, memories, and recommendations that only Tobias
   can provide.
3. **The Joshua's Point way to experience it** — a professionally edited journey that helps guests
   use their time well without turning the day into a list of attractions.

These layers must remain identifiable during research, editing, and review. General context must
not be written as Tobias's memory. Personal experience must never be inferred from generic source
material. The recommended journey may combine verified information and Tobias's experience, but it
must explain the reasoning behind its sequence.

The result should be a professionally curated local guide with lived experience as its distinctive
value. It must not become an encyclopedia of places, a personal diary, or a generic travel blog.

### Source hierarchy

Every chapter and journey must follow this order. A lower-priority source fills a genuine gap; it
does not replace useful material from a higher-priority source.

1. **Existing Southern Negros Guide** — `https://guide.joshuaspoint.com/`
   - Primary migration source for destination information, practical information, local
     background, and existing editorial text.
   - Preserve useful structure and Joshua's Point knowledge. Improve clarity and pacing without
     rewriting strong material merely to make it different.
2. **Existing Joshua's Point website**
   - Source for destination pages, scenic routes, dive guides, photography references, maps, and
     established relationships among places.
   - Reuse canonical website and CMS records rather than copying their facts into the premium
     layer.
3. **Approved Tobias owner source**
   - Source for personal experience, memories, recommendations, observations, opinions, and the
     Joshua's Point way of shaping a journey.
   - This is the distinctive premium layer. It must remain clearly separated from general place
     information.
4. **Verified public sources**
   - Use only when factual context remains missing after reviewing the existing guide and website.
   - Limit use to history, nature, culture, and geography.
   - Record source and review details internally.

Verified public sources must never be used to infer or invent Tobias's experience,
recommendations, memories, opinions, visits, or feelings. Existing Joshua's Point material should
be migrated and improved before new text is commissioned.

### Required source balance in every chapter

Every chapter should contain these six editorial functions where relevant:

1. **The Place** — led by the existing Southern Negros Guide, supported by existing Joshua's Point
   material and verified public context only when a factual gap remains.
2. **Why We Recommend It** — approved Tobias source.
3. **The Journey** — existing guide and route knowledge shaped by Tobias's approved experience and
   recommendation.
4. **Local Perspective** — Tobias only.
5. **Practical guidance** — existing guide material retained where current, then checked against
   verified facts and canonical public records.
6. **Premium recommendations** — Tobias's approved selection, sequencing, preparation, and small
   details that help guests experience the journey better.

If a required source layer is missing, record the gap. Do not replace it with generic travel copy.

## 3. Premium journey format v3

Every full premium journey should follow the same information logic while allowing its visual and
narrative rhythm to vary. The format is not a page-builder template and should not make every
journey look identical.

The journey title, route summary, edition information, and review date sit before this structure as
publication metadata. The editorial journey itself follows the twelve sections below.

### 1. The Place

#### Purpose

Give the reader enough verified context to understand the place before receiving a recommendation.

#### Include

- Geographic and environmental context relevant to the journey.
- Nature, culture, community, or history when it materially helps understanding.
- Why the place is known, stated without rankings or exaggerated claims.
- Useful existing Joshua's Point material that already explains the place well.

#### Sources and boundaries

- Use verified external sources and existing Joshua's Point content.
- Record sources and review dates internally.
- Keep changing practical information out of this section.
- Do not convert general knowledge into Tobias's voice or imply that he personally witnessed it.
- Leave disputed or insufficiently sourced context out.

### 2. Why We Recommend It

#### Purpose

State the Joshua's Point point of view clearly and personally.

#### Include

- Why Tobias recommends giving this place part of a guest's stay.
- What he believes makes the experience worth the time or effort.
- Why the place fits the Joshua's Point way of discovering Southern Negros.
- The part of the experience that matters most to him.

This section requires approved Tobias source material. It cannot be constructed from external
research or generic destination copy.

### 3. The Journey

#### Purpose

Show how the experience unfolds as a considered day rather than a minute-by-minute itinerary.

#### Include

1. The start at Joshua's Point.
2. The road, crossing, or other transition.
3. Meaningful pauses.
4. The central experience.
5. A shorter, quieter, or weather-dependent alternative where one genuinely exists.
6. The return.

Use existing verified routes, maps, and content relationships. Explain the emotional rhythm—the
change between road and water, effort and pause, anticipation and arrival—without inventing timing,
conditions, or certainty.

### 4. Local Perspective

#### Purpose

Carry the premium value that cannot be reproduced by general research.

#### Include

- Tobias's first-hand observations and memories.
- Details he noticed during real visits.
- Personal recommendations and honest limitations.
- The small part of the experience he still remembers afterward.

Use **I** only when Tobias has approved the first-person observation. Preserve his meaning and
natural specificity while editing grammar, readability, and pacing. Never invent a visit, memory,
feeling, recommendation, or personal detail.

### 5. Who Will Love It

- Describe the guest, interest, and pace that fit the journey.
- Set honest expectations about what the day emphasizes.
- Include families, divers, swimmers, walkers, or mixed-interest groups only when supported.
- Do not imply universal suitability or use unsupported labels such as “for everyone.”

### 6. Prepare For The Day

- Describe observed physical requirements and relevant practical boundaries.
- Explain weather, sea, road, heat, stairs, water, or changing conditions where verified or clearly
  framed as something to reconfirm.
- State who needs to make an individual assessment.
- Keep warnings proportionate, visible, and calm.
- Do not assign unsupported difficulty ratings or make safety guarantees.

### 7. What To Bring

- Use Tobias's journey-specific recommendations where available.
- Explain why an item matters when that adds practical value.
- Separate personal advice from requirements imposed by an operator, authority, or site.
- Avoid generic packing filler repeated across every journey.

### 8. Small Details That Make It Better

- Timing suggestions supported by Tobias's experience.
- Natural combinations or pauses that improve the rhythm of the day.
- Small choices that reduce unnecessary effort or help guests pay attention.
- A quieter alternative where Tobias genuinely recommends one.

Do not promise crowd levels, operating conditions, wildlife, weather, or availability. This section
should save the guest time without encouraging them to maximize the number of stops.

### 9. Map

- Verified overview, coordinates, route relationships, and ordered stops.
- Clear orientation, attribution, edition date, and limitations.
- Accessible text sequence for readers who do not use the map.
- Public update link or QR code where appropriate.
- No private coordinates, invented tracks, or unreviewed technical dive locations.
- An editorial connection may show a journey relationship, such as a sea crossing, only when it is
  clearly distinguished from a navigational GPS track.

### 10. Photography

- Opening landscape or arrival image.
- Images that support the journey sequence.
- One or two details that reveal the character Tobias describes.
- Current usable assets and the photography still required.
- Accurate caption and alt-text source notes.
- Photographer, rights, approval, crop, focal point, and development/production status.

People require consent. No photograph may imply a place, activity, weather condition, wildlife
sighting, or view it does not actually show.

### 11. Related Places

- Connect relevant destinations, scenic routes, dive guides, gateways, and nearby experiences.
- Explain why a relationship is useful instead of presenting an automatic list.
- Prefer a small number of meaningful relationships.
- Do not repeat full destination content inside the journey.
- Keep premium sequencing distinct from the free public profiles it references.

### 12. Practical Notes

- Include only verified information that helps the guest act confidently.
- Separate stable context from information that must be reconfirmed.
- Where relevant, cover access, fees, registration, current arrangements, local rules, and
  specialist briefings only after verification.
- State the source and last-reviewed date internally.
- Use the current public page as the canonical correction and update surface when appropriate.
- Leave a field empty rather than infer a fact from a map, old page, or personal memory.

### Journey title and publication metadata

- Name the journey in guest language.
- Use place names naturally.
- Avoid rankings, superlatives, and generic adventure language.

The journey header and production record should also carry, internally and where appropriate
visibly:

- Owner source.
- General context sources.
- Fact reviewer.
- Dive or specialist reviewer.
- Last reviewed date.
- Map version.
- Photography credits.
- Canonical public URL.

### Editorial balance check

Before a journey is approved, the editor must confirm:

- **Place knowledge:** Can a new reader understand where they are going and why the place matters?
- **Personal value:** Is Tobias's lived observation present and clearly distinguished from general
  fact?
- **Recommended experience:** Does the sequence help a guest use the day better?
- **Practical confidence:** Are actionable notes verified, current, and proportionate?
- **Restraint:** Has the journey avoided becoming an encyclopedia entry, diary, directory, or travel
  blog?

A journey is incomplete if any one of the first three layers is absent. Missing owner experience
must remain an explicit editorial gap; it may not be filled by generic writing.

## 4. Edition 1 writing style

### Voice

The guide should sound like Tobias quietly showing someone a place he knows and loves.

The voice is:

- Personal, but not confessional.
- Grounded in real observation.
- Warm without selling.
- Calm without becoming vague.
- Curious without collecting attractions.
- Confident about lived experience and humble about uncertainty.

### Point of view

- Use **we** when the recommendation genuinely reflects Joshua's Point or a shared journey.
- Use **I** only for an approved first-hand Tobias observation.
- Use **you** sparingly for clear practical guidance, not persuasion.
- Name the source when specialist or external knowledge is involved.

### Preferred specificity

Prefer:

- The sound, surface, weather, light, road, effort, pause, and sequence Tobias actually remembers.
- Why one stop belongs before or after another.
- What needs preparation.
- What can change.
- The choice to do less.

Avoid replacing those details with summary adjectives.

### Sentence rhythm

- Use clear sentences with natural variation.
- Let reflective passages breathe.
- Keep practical information direct and scannable.
- Use headings to orient, not to fragment every paragraph.
- Avoid repeating the same opening formula on every journey.

### Headlines

Headlines should be observant and place-led. They may carry a quiet idea, but they should not hide
the place name or imitate magazine clickbait.

Avoid:

- “The ultimate...”
- “The best...”
- “Top things to do...”
- “A hidden gem...”
- “An unforgettable adventure...”
- “Paradise awaits...”

### Travel language to avoid

- Breathtaking.
- Bucket list.
- Exclusive.
- Hidden paradise.
- Luxury escape.
- Must-see, unless preserved inside a clearly attributed owner quotation and editorially justified.
- Once-in-a-lifetime.
- World-class.
- Perfect for everyone.

### Factual language

Separate clearly:

- **Owner observation:** what Tobias remembers, notices, or recommends.
- **Verified fact:** checked information with a source and review date.
- **Qualified guidance:** specialist-reviewed material.
- **Changeable information:** something the reader must reconfirm.

Do not convert personal memory into a universal fact. Do not soften a limitation because it makes
the journey less attractive.

### Practical writing

- Explain what a fact means for the day.
- Use metric units and locally appropriate currency when verified.
- State what must be reconfirmed.
- Avoid exactness that cannot be maintained.
- Keep warnings proportionate, visible, and calm.

### Editorial restraint

If an existing passage already sounds like Tobias and communicates something specific, preserve it
and improve only grammar, clarity, or pacing. Do not rewrite strong source material merely to make
the new guide sound different.

### Privacy

Never publish:

- The private meaning behind the Joshua name.
- Private family stories or relationships without separate approval.
- Exact private property coordinates.
- Internal verification notes.
- Personal contact information not approved for public use.

## 5. Photography and map standards

### Photography principle

Photography proves and deepens the story. It must not decorate an unsupported claim.

For every selected image record:

- Filename or asset reference.
- Actual subject and location.
- Photographer and rights.
- Intended chapter and editorial purpose.
- Caption source.
- Alt-text notes.
- Crop and focal-point guidance.
- Development or production status.

Use existing owner photography during production. Mark development selections as temporary and
replaceable. Never use stock photography or misidentify a place.

### Map principle

Maps provide orientation and relationship, not guaranteed navigation.

Every Edition 1 map requires:

- Verified coordinates or owner-approved route geometry.
- Text alternative.
- Public/private location review.
- Attribution.
- Edition and review date.
- Clear legend and readable labels.
- Public corrections URL.
- Light and print contrast testing.

No map should introduce a place or route that the chapter does not explain.

## 6. Production workflow

The production path is:

> Source content → edit → photography → maps → PDF layout → review

Each arrow is a quality gate. Work may overlap, but no stage should silently repair missing source
or verification from an earlier stage.

### Stage 1 — Source content

**Inputs**

- Existing guide pages.
- Current Joshua's Point Sanity documents.
- Approved owner-source records.
- Existing scenic-route and map data.
- Owner photography and captions.
- Qualified specialist sources where required.

**Actions**

1. Create a source packet for each chapter and journey.
2. Preserve the original passage and URL before editing.
3. Sort material into verified place knowledge, Tobias's personal experience, and the Joshua's
   Point recommended way to experience the place.
4. Label owner observation, verified fact, specialist guidance, and changeable information within
   those layers.
5. Record missing owner input and blocked claims.
6. Remove excluded geographic and commercial material from the working packet.

**Gate**

No drafting begins until the source of every substantive claim is visible.

### Stage 2 — Editorial edit

**Actions**

1. Preserve strong Tobias passages.
2. Improve grammar, readability, rhythm, sequence, and repetition.
3. Assemble destinations into complete journeys using the Premium Journey Format v3.
4. Keep general place context, local perspective, and the recommended journey distinct but
   mutually supportive.
5. Separate story from verified practical guidance.
6. Remove generic travel language and unsupported claims.
7. Keep missing facts or owner experience empty or explicitly blocked.
8. Review privacy boundaries.

**Reviews**

- Editor review.
- Tobias meaning-and-voice review.
- Fact review.
- Qualified dive/specialist review where applicable.

**Gate**

The text is approved in substance before final photography or map composition begins. The journey
must contain all three editorial layers: an understood place, genuine Tobias perspective, and a
useful Joshua's Point way to experience it.

### Stage 3 — Photography

**Actions**

1. Match each approved section to a required editorial image role.
2. Select truthful owner photography first.
3. Record missing images without delaying text work.
4. Confirm rights, credits, captions, and people consent.
5. Prepare crop and focal-point guidance for portrait PDF use.
6. Mark development imagery for replacement.

**Gate**

Every placed image has an accurate identity, purpose, credit, and production status.

### Stage 4 — Maps

**Actions**

1. Pull verified coordinates and route geometry from canonical sources.
2. Produce chapter and journey maps from the existing provider-neutral architecture/data.
3. Write the accessible text sequence.
4. Review privacy, dive-location, and safety boundaries.
5. Add attribution, version, review date, and public correction link.
6. Test labels at phone and print sizes.

**Gate**

Every marker and route is verified; every map remains understandable without interaction.

### Stage 5 — PDF layout

**Actions**

1. Apply the Joshua's Point typography, color, spacing, and image principles.
2. Build a calm portrait layout that works first on small screens.
3. Establish chapter openings, journey navigation, practical callouts, maps, captions, and indexes.
4. Preserve semantic heading order and tagged reading structure.
5. Add bookmarks, internal links, descriptive external links, and page references.
6. Optimize images without destroying print or screen quality.
7. Generate the separate map pack from the same approved map sources.

**Gate**

The PDF is complete, internally consistent, accessible, and usable offline before commercial setup.

### Stage 6 — Review and release candidate

**Editorial review**

- Tobias voice and meaning.
- No generic marketing language.
- No private information.
- No duplicated or contradictory facts.

**Factual review**

- Dates, access, fees, transport, services, and maps.
- Dive and specialist material.
- Public corrections links.

**Visual review**

- Photography identity and sequence.
- Crops, captions, maps, indexes, and page rhythm.
- Joshua's Point brand consistency.

**Accessibility review**

- Tagged PDF structure.
- Reading order.
- Heading hierarchy.
- Contrast.
- Link purpose.
- Image descriptions.
- Keyboard navigation and bookmarks.

**Device and field review**

- Phone.
- Tablet.
- Laptop.
- Common PDF readers.
- Print sample.
- Offline use.
- Realistic file download conditions.

**Owner acceptance test**

Give the release candidate to representative readers and ask them to plan one complete day without
additional explanation. Record where they hesitate, misunderstand the map, miss a confirmation, or
cannot distinguish observation from fact.

**Release gate**

Do not release until:

- Tobias approves the complete editorial meaning.
- All required reviewers sign off.
- Every included map and photograph is approved.
- Blocked or stale facts are removed or corrected.
- The corrections, license, delivery, refund, and update policies are ready.
- A real purchase and download test succeeds after commerce is implemented.

### Edition archive

For every release preserve:

- Final source packets.
- Approved manuscript.
- Photography manifest.
- Map manifest and attribution.
- Review record.
- PDF source and final file.
- File checksum.
- Edition number and changelog.
- Superseded-file status.

This archive protects future Tobias and prevents Edition 2 from repeating Edition 1 research.
