# Joshua's Point — The House Photography Brief

**Document type:** Internal photography planning reference
**Route supported:** `/the-house`
**Status:** Pre-production brief; locations, facts, and lived moments require owner confirmation
**Public website copy:** No

## Purpose

This brief translates the approved House editorial structure into a truthful photography plan. The
photographs should help a future guest understand the relationship among house, land, weather, and
ordinary life. They should not present the property as an object of status or make spaces appear
larger, emptier, or more perfect than they are.

The three protected feelings are the test for every image:

- **Homy:** lived in, welcoming, and recognizably used without exposing private life.
- **Connected to nature:** architecture helps the viewer notice land, sea, mountains, vegetation,
  light, air, and weather.
- **Peaceful:** calm observation, visual restraint, and enough space for the scene to breathe.

This is not permission to stage or publish an unverified story. Suggested viewpoints must be
confirmed on site. Captions must be written only after the depicted place, time, weather, visible
geography, and activity have been verified.

## Working principles

### Photography is evidence

Each retained photograph should reveal something that another image does not. The current page can
hold nine principal images, but that is capacity rather than a quota. Fewer distinct, truthful
images are preferable to a complete sequence built through repetition.

### Nature remains the hero

The house may frame the image, but architecture should lead attention toward lived relationships
with the setting. Avoid façade cataloguing, isolated amenity images, exaggerated skies, and aerial
spectacle.

### Real use, not performance

Ordinary signs of life may remain when they are truthful and privacy-safe. Do not manufacture a
breakfast, gathering, rainy-day routine, or evening scene solely for the camera. If a moment is
re-created for practical reasons, it must still represent confirmed ordinary use.

### Caption and alt-text roles

- **Caption:** optional public context. It may identify a verified place, viewpoint, time, weather,
  material, or activity when that information adds editorial value.
- **Alt text:** describes the meaningful visual information in the final crop. It should not repeat
  the caption, begin with “image of,” assign emotions to viewers, identify people unnecessarily, or
  state geography and materials that have not been verified.
- If an image is purely atmospheric and adjacent text already provides all its information, the
  accessibility review may classify it as decorative. Editorially meaningful photographs should
  normally receive useful alt text.

For every capture, retain internal notes for photographer, rights, consent, date, approximate time,
weather, viewpoint, visible landmarks, orientation, and intended crop.

## 1. Hero — House within the landscape

### Purpose

Establish the house as part of a wider setting before presenting its rooms or details. This image
must have a different job from **The View**: the Hero shows the relationship between land and house;
The View looks outward from a real position at the house.

### Emotional goal

Connected to nature first, with a quiet sense of arrival. The building should feel inhabitable and
grounded rather than monumental.

### Suggested viewpoint

A human-height position from which both the setting and enough of the house are honestly visible.
Confirm the actual approach and the owner's arrival experience before selecting the final position.

### Orientation

Panoramic or wide landscape. Capture additional width and vertical allowance for responsive crops,
with a recorded mobile focal point.

### Recommended composition

- Let land, vegetation, weather, sea, or mountains occupy meaningful visual space where genuinely
  visible.
- Keep the house within context rather than centering an isolated façade.
- Use natural perspective and restrained color.
- Preserve calm negative space without depending on a text overlay.
- Show ordinary conditions representative of the place.

### What to avoid

- Wide-angle or elevated distortion that makes the house appear larger.
- Aerial spectacle as the defining first impression.
- Perfect-weather imagery presented as the only condition.
- Security-sensitive access details or precise location clues that the owner has not approved.
- Claims about slope, ridge, sea, islands, mountains, orientation, or design response before
  verification.

### Caption and alt-text considerations

- Caption only if a verified viewpoint or landscape relationship adds necessary context.
- Alt text should describe the visible relationship between the house and setting in the selected
  crop.
- Name bodies of water, islands, mountains, towns, or landforms only after verification.

## 2. Shared Heart of the House

### Purpose

Show how the kitchen, dining area, living room, deck, and outside belong to one lived composition,
as far as a truthful viewpoint permits.

### Emotional goal

Homy and quietly social. The image should make shared time imaginable without presenting a party,
event, or staged hospitality scene.

### Suggested viewpoint

A natural standing or seated position used in daily life, looking across more than one shared zone
and, where possible, toward the deck or landscape. Confirm the real spatial relationship before the
shoot.

### Orientation

Landscape for the principal image. An optional secondary detail may be portrait or landscape only
if it adds a distinct lived observation.

### Recommended composition

- Show connection and human scale rather than maximum room coverage.
- Allow ordinary, approved signs of cooking, eating, reading, or conversation to remain.
- Use open or closed doors only as they would genuinely be used in the recorded conditions.
- Balance interior warmth with the conditions outside rather than exposing for only one plane.

### What to avoid

- Ultra-wide compositions intended to exaggerate size.
- Showroom styling, immaculate emptiness, or decorative objects with private significance.
- Event-scale groups, staged celebration, or implied party use.
- A photograph claiming all spaces connect when the viewpoint does not demonstrate that.

### Caption and alt-text considerations

- Verify the rooms and exterior area actually visible before naming them.
- A caption may record a public-safe, owner-confirmed use of the shared space; it must not invent a
  gathering.
- Alt text should describe the spatial relationship and meaningful activity without identifying
  people unless identity is editorially necessary and approved.

## 3. The View

### Purpose

Provide the page's longest visual pause and let the outward landscape stand without architectural
explanation.

### Emotional goal

Peaceful and attentive. The image should show why the view becomes part of ordinary life rather
than present scenery as an attraction.

### Suggested viewpoint

An identifiable, regularly experienced position within or immediately around the house. Record the
exact camera position internally so visible geography and future recaptures can be verified.

### Orientation

Panoramic landscape, composed for a wide editorial field with a safe narrower crop.

### Recommended composition

- Use an honest focal length and level horizon.
- Include only foreground architecture that helps locate the viewer's real position.
- Prefer representative natural light and restrained post-processing.
- Let weather and distance remain nuanced; do not force clarity that was not present.

### What to avoid

- An indistinguishable repeat of the Hero composition.
- Telephoto compression or panorama stitching that materially changes perceived relationships.
- Replaced skies, excessive saturation, removed weather, or artificial sun effects.
- Unverified landmark labels or sunrise/sunset implications.

### Caption and alt-text considerations

- Internally record date, time, weather, direction, camera position, and candidate landmarks.
- Public captions should name only confirmed geography and should remain optional.
- Alt text should describe what is visibly significant in the final crop, including weather when
  relevant.

## 4. Indoor/Outdoor Relationship — Open threshold

### Purpose

Show the physical and lived transition between an interior room and the outdoors, including the
role of sliding doors where verified.

### Emotional goal

Connected to nature through an ordinary threshold, not through an abstract architectural claim.

### Suggested viewpoint

At or just beside a threshold, at human height, with both interior and exterior retaining readable
detail. Confirm which doors are normally opened and under which conditions.

### Orientation

Portrait is preferred for the controlled essay, but landscape is acceptable if the real
relationship cannot be represented honestly in a vertical frame.

### Recommended composition

- Let the frame move naturally from interior surface to opening to outside condition.
- Preserve the depth and actual scale of the threshold.
- Show openness or shelter as it existed during the capture.
- Include weather traces or movement only when genuinely observed.

### What to avoid

- Opening doors solely for the photograph when that would be unrepresentative.
- Hiding tracks, screens, edges, or practical weather boundaries to idealize the concept.
- Claims about cooling, ventilation, shade, or “seamlessness” without evidence.
- Security-sensitive details about door operation or access.

### Caption and alt-text considerations

- Caption may identify the connected spaces after spatial verification.
- Alt text should explain the visible transition; it should not claim environmental performance.
- Do not describe air movement, temperature, or routine unless the photograph and approved source
  support it.

## 5. Deck and roof

### Purpose

Show how the house creates a place between exposure and shelter, and how the deck participates in
daily life.

### Emotional goal

Peaceful protection without separating people from weather or landscape.

### Suggested viewpoint

A position beneath or beside the roof edge that shows deck surface, shelter, and outside condition
in relationship. A secondary detail may focus on a verified material or weather response.

### Orientation

Portrait or restrained detail for the primary essay role. Capture a wider reference frame for
verification even if it is not published.

### Recommended composition

- Include enough context to understand what the roof shelters and where the deck sits.
- Show honest shade, rain, light, or weathering when naturally present.
- Keep lines and perspective true to the building.
- Let use and atmosphere matter more than construction display.

### What to avoid

- Inferring roof purpose, drainage behavior, thermal effect, or structural design from appearance.
- Naming deck or roof materials without records or owner/architect confirmation.
- Detail so abstract that the relationship with the landscape is lost.
- Removing ordinary weathering to suggest untouched finishes.

### Caption and alt-text considerations

- Verify roof and deck material, location, intended function, and depicted condition before
  captioning.
- Alt text should describe visible shelter and surfaces, not presumed performance.
- Avoid architectural credit until spelling, role, and permission are confirmed.

## 6. Pool relationship

### Purpose

Show the pool as one part of the house–deck–landscape composition rather than as an isolated
amenity.

### Emotional goal

Calm continuity among water, architecture, people, and the setting.

### Suggested viewpoint

A real circulation or resting position from which the house, deck, pool, and surrounding landscape
can be read together. Confirm the actual relationship before choosing the frame.

### Orientation

Landscape.

### Recommended composition

- Give the pool context on more than one side where possible.
- Preserve natural scale and the actual horizon relationship.
- Include use only if it is ordinary, consented, and visually quiet.
- Treat reflections and changing weather naturally.

### What to avoid

- A freestanding pool feature shot.
- Resort conventions such as staged loungers, cocktails, towels, or aspirational posing.
- Lens or camera height choices that imply an unverified infinity relationship.
- Claims about dimensions, depth, heating, treatment, safety, or availability.

### Caption and alt-text considerations

- Caption may explain a verified physical or lived relationship, not advertise the pool.
- Alt text should describe how the pool sits relative to visible house, deck, and setting.
- Do not include pool specifications until owner records confirm them and publication is necessary.

## 7. Morning

### Purpose

Document how an actual morning begins at Joshua's Point through light, weather, a room, or an
owner-confirmed routine.

### Emotional goal

Homy and quietly awake; attentive rather than idealized.

### Suggested viewpoint

The place where morning is genuinely first noticed or ordinarily spent, to be selected after owner
source gathering. Record direction and time rather than assuming sunrise orientation.

### Orientation

Landscape or portrait according to the confirmed moment. The present layout supports landscape,
but truth takes priority over symmetry.

### Recommended composition

- Photograph existing light and an ordinary level of activity.
- Include a simple trace of routine only when it is owner-confirmed and privacy-safe.
- Let exterior conditions remain present through openings or reflected light where natural.

### What to avoid

- Manufactured breakfast scenes or generic coffee imagery.
- Calling light “first light,” sunrise, or east-facing without verification.
- Over-warming white balance or staging a perfect-weather morning.
- Presenting a personal family ritual as a typical guest experience.

### Caption and alt-text considerations

- Record capture time, weather, room or location, and observed activity.
- Caption only an approved observation.
- Alt text should describe the light, setting, and visible activity without promising a routine.

## 8. Rain

### Purpose

Represent rain as a real condition of the house, including atmosphere and practical boundaries,
rather than hiding it or romanticizing it.

### Emotional goal

Sheltered, connected, and peaceful while remaining honest about weather.

### Suggested viewpoint

A safe, normally occupied sheltered position from which rain, an edge of the house, and the outside
can be understood together. Confirm how the space is actually used during rain.

### Orientation

Portrait or landscape according to the strongest verified relationship. The controlled daily
sequence may accept fewer moments if the rain image is not ready.

### Recommended composition

- Show visible rain through surfaces, atmosphere, runoff, vegetation, or water where naturally
  legible.
- Include shelter and exposure in the same visual story where possible.
- Preserve the real light level and color of the weather.
- Record practical effects separately for verification.

### What to avoid

- Artificial rain, added rain effects, or darkening solely for drama.
- Describing drainage, access, power, deck safety, or weather performance from one image.
- Presenting rain only as spectacle or erasing its practical consequences.
- Unsafe camera positions or activity during severe weather.

### Caption and alt-text considerations

- Record date, time, intensity as observed without unsupported classification, and camera shelter.
- Caption may describe a confirmed experience or condition without generalizing a season.
- Alt text should make the visible rain and shelter relationship understandable.

## 9. Evening

### Purpose

Show the transition from outside light to the inhabited interior and close the daily sequence
without relying on a generic sunset.

### Emotional goal

Warm, homy, and settled.

### Suggested viewpoint

A normally used evening position that includes both interior presence and the changing exterior,
selected after the owner describes where evenings are actually spent.

### Orientation

Portrait or landscape according to the confirmed moment. Avoid forcing the same composition as
Morning.

### Recommended composition

- Balance natural remaining light with the house's real evening lighting.
- Allow windows, open thresholds, or exterior darkness to retain detail where possible.
- Include quiet shared presence only with consent and when representative.
- Preserve the ordinary character of the room.

### What to avoid

- Generic sunset-only scenery detached from the house.
- Excessive interior lighting, staged candles, or artificial warmth.
- Claims about sunset direction, visible geography, silence, or nightly routine without evidence.
- Private family objects or identifiable moments not approved for publication.

### Caption and alt-text considerations

- Record location, approximate time, weather, actual lighting, and any visible geography.
- Caption an approved observation rather than assigning an emotional outcome.
- Alt text should describe the relationship between interior and exterior in the final crop.

## 10. Materials and details

### Purpose

Make verified parts of the house tactile and understandable through use, light, weathering, and
care—not through specifications or status.

### Emotional goal

Homy through touch and Connected to nature through honest aging and environmental context.

### Suggested viewpoint

Close, human-scale observations of confirmed materials or construction details in their actual
location. Capture a wider context frame for internal identification.

### Orientation

Portrait, landscape detail, or restrained sequence. These images are optional and should not
displace the landscape story.

### Recommended composition

- Photograph only after material name, application, and location are confirmed.
- Show touch, joinery, wear, shadow, water, or weathering when editorially meaningful.
- Keep color and texture natural.
- Connect detail to lived use rather than isolating it as a product sample.

### What to avoid

- Assuming timber species, stone type, sourcing, craft method, or sustainability.
- Pristine finish photography that conceals normal use.
- Generic decoration without architectural or lived relevance.
- Treating “Light” or “Air” as materials without an approved editorial decision.

### Caption and alt-text considerations

- Internal notes must include verified material name, exact location, source, and condition.
- Public captions should use approved common-language terms unless technical specificity helps the
  reader and has been verified.
- Alt text should describe visible texture, use, and context; it should not infer provenance or
  performance.

## 11. Optional human moments

### Purpose

Introduce human scale and genuine shared life where it strengthens the House story. Human presence
is optional; the page must not depend on exposing private people to feel homy.

### Emotional goal

Welcoming, natural, and unperformed.

### Suggested viewpoint

An observational position that respects personal space and keeps the house and nature in the story.
Prefer gestures, shared activity, or figures at a distance when identity is not necessary.

### Orientation

Choose according to the confirmed moment. Capture alternatives that do not require identifiable
faces.

### Recommended composition

- Photograph only an activity that genuinely belongs to the house.
- Keep groups small and consistent with the peaceful, non-party identity.
- Obtain explicit consent and record agreed channels, duration, territories, and withdrawal
  arrangements before publication.
- Consider non-identifying hands, silhouettes, backs, or distant figures where appropriate, while
  recognizing that these can still be identifiable.

### What to avoid

- Staged models implying a guest experience that has not occurred.
- Private family memories used as public emotional proof.
- Minors without guardian permission and a specific safeguarding decision.
- Event-scale scenes, aspirational posing, alcohol-led imagery, or spectacle.
- Publishing identifiable guests, staff, neighbours, or family members through assumed consent.

### Caption and alt-text considerations

- Do not name people unless necessary and explicitly approved.
- Captions must not convert a private event into a representative guest story.
- Alt text should describe relevant action and setting without unnecessary identity, age, ethnicity,
  relationship, emotion, or disability inference.

## Minimum readiness before page integration

- Hero and View have distinct, approved images and purposes.
- Shared Heart has one photograph that truthfully demonstrates the selected relationship.
- Indoor/Outdoor has at least two distinct approved photographs and one sourced observation.
- Daily Rhythms renders only moments supported by both photography and owner source.
- Materials photography is used only for confirmed entries.
- Every published asset has rights, privacy, crop, caption, and alt-text decisions recorded.
- The complete sequence has been reviewed on mobile and desktop for repetition, visual density, and
  page length.
