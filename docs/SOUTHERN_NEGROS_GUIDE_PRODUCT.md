# Joshua's Point — Southern Negros Guide Product

## Status and scope

This document defines the product, commercial, delivery, and launch architecture for the Southern
Negros Guide. It does not select a payment provider, create a checkout, define CMS schemas, or
change the website.

The guide is a paid editorial companion to the public Joshua's Point travel guide. It should make
careful regional knowledge easier to carry, save, print, and use offline. It must never turn the
website into a tourism marketplace or place essential safety information behind a paywall.

Payment fees and provider capabilities in Section 9 were checked against official provider
documentation on **9 August 2026**. They are decision inputs, not contractual quotes. Eligibility,
fees, settlement currencies, tax treatment, and product approval must be confirmed again before a
provider is selected or a checkout is implemented.

## Product principles

1. **The region comes first.** The guide exists to help people understand southern Negros, not to
   create another list of attractions.
2. **Paid means more useful, not more persuasive.** Customers pay for curation, synthesis, offline
   access, considered routes, and maintained editions.
3. **Essential knowledge remains public.** Safety corrections, access closures, and responsible
   travel guidance must not depend on a purchase.
4. **Facts are dated.** Travel times, conditions, opening patterns, fees, and access information
   must carry a reviewed date and be presented as changeable.
5. **Local knowledge is credited.** Contributors, photographers, reviewers, and community sources
   are acknowledged and compensated where appropriate.
6. **Independent travel is supported responsibly.** A guide complements current local advice,
   official notices, qualified dive professionals, and a traveller's own judgment.
7. **The product stays quiet.** No urgency devices, artificial scarcity, rankings, paid placement,
   or inflated promises.

## 1. Vision

The Southern Negros Guide should become the most thoughtful independent companion to exploring the
region from Joshua's Point: locally grounded, visually calm, practical in the field, and written
with the attention of a small travel journal.

The guide should help a reader understand not only where to go, but how a day might unfold: the
road out in the morning, the weather across the hills, where to pause, what to bring, when to turn
back, and how to arrive with respect. It should reward time and curiosity rather than encourage a
traveller to collect places.

### Product promise

> A considered guide to moving through southern Negros slowly, independently, and with care.

### What the product is

- An editorially curated and dated regional guide.
- A practical offline companion before and during a journey.
- A bridge between Joshua's Point, the public website, and the wider region.
- A maintained publication with clear editions and corrections.

### What the product is not

- A booking platform, tour operator, dive operator, navigation service, or emergency resource.
- A real-time source of road, weather, marine, transport, price, or opening information.
- A complete directory or a guarantee of access, safety, conditions, or availability.
- A paid-ranking product for local businesses.

### Success

The product succeeds when readers feel better oriented, travel at a more considered pace, and can
make informed choices without being pushed toward more activity. Commercially, it should support
the cost of research, verification, photography, editing, and updates without changing the voice
of Joshua's Point.

## 2. Target audience

### Primary audiences

#### Joshua's Point guests

Guests planning before arrival or deciding how to spend an unhurried day from the house. They need
a trusted starting point, realistic journey context, and material that works with inconsistent
mobile coverage.

#### Independent southern Negros travellers

Visitors who may not stay at Joshua's Point but share its way of travelling: small groups, couples,
solo travellers, repeat visitors, and people who prefer context over itineraries built around
volume.

#### Interest-led travellers

- Divers who want a well-edited regional overview before speaking with qualified local operators.
- Experienced scooter riders who need route character and practical preparation, not turn-by-turn
  certainty.
- Food-curious travellers who care about producers, markets, everyday cooking, and local context.
- Architecture, landscape, and photography readers who may use the guide as a publication as much
  as a field tool.

### Audience needs

| Need | Product response |
| --- | --- |
| Confidence before leaving | Dated practical notes, route character, preparation, and limitations |
| Use without reliable signal | Downloadable, mobile-readable, printable editions |
| Less research fragmentation | One edited view with links back to current public sources |
| Regional context | Essays, photography, maps, and connections between places |
| Honest expectations | Clear unknowns, seasonal variation, and last-reviewed dates |
| Accessibility | Tagged PDFs, readable type, strong structure, descriptive links, and text alternatives |

### People the guide should not pretend to serve

The guide is not sufficient on its own for novice riders, technical diving decisions, emergency
planning, accessibility guarantees, or journeys whose conditions require a current professional
assessment. Sales copy and product pages must say this plainly.

## 3. Free vs Premium strategy

The free website and paid guide should reinforce one another. The public guide builds trust and
keeps essential information reachable. The paid product packages deeper editorial work and field
utility.

### Free public guide

The website should continue to provide:

- Complete introductory destination and dive-site pages where published.
- A meaningful sample of the editorial story, not deliberately weakened excerpts.
- Essential access, conduct, and safety notices.
- Public corrections and closure notices.
- Basic orientation, relationships, and onward reading.
- Clear last-reviewed dates.
- A representative sample or preview of each paid guide.

### Paid guide

The paid edition may add:

- A coherent, professionally edited collection rather than separate web pages.
- Offline and print-ready formats.
- Deeper route narratives and practical preparation.
- Curated day shapes and thematic collections without rigid itineraries.
- Original commissioned photography, diagrams, and map layouts where licensed.
- Cross-references that work in print and offline.
- Downloadable route files only after their safety and support model is approved.
- Defined access to corrections and edition updates.

### Ethical boundary

Premium value must come from **curation, portability, depth, and maintenance**. Joshua's Point
should never withhold a known hazard, closure, ecological restriction, or material correction to
create purchase value.

### Recommended access model for launch

- Sell each guide as a one-time purchase.
- Do not require an account if secure receipt-based access is sufficient.
- Give buyers the current edition plus the update entitlement stated at purchase.
- Consider including the Explorer Guide with qualifying Joshua's Point stays only after the guest
  entitlement and support workflow are defined.
- Keep marketing consent separate from receipts and product-critical update messages.

## 4. Product versions

All versions share one publication system: consistent navigation, visual language, fact labels,
review dates, accessibility standards, licensing, and correction workflow. Each must still have a
clear reason to exist on its own.

### Explorer Guide

**Role:** The broad first guide and recommended product to validate the format.

**Editorial territory:** Landscape, towns, waterfalls, lakes, islands, viewpoints, beaches,
culture, coffee stops, and the rhythm of moving between them.

**Core contents:**

- Regional orientation and how to use the guide.
- A small, deliberately selected collection of destinations.
- Journey character and approximate time from Joshua's Point.
- Day shapes such as a nearby morning or a longer coastal day.
- Preparation, local etiquette, responsible access, and seasonal context.
- Offline map overview and links to the latest public pages.

**Boundary:** It is not an exhaustive destination directory.

### Dive Guide

**Role:** An editorial regional overview that helps divers have better-informed conversations with
qualified operators.

**Editorial territory:** Dive regions and sites, marine habitats, seasonal context, photography,
and the relationship between the coast and nearby destinations.

**Core contents:**

- Regional orientation and glossary.
- Editorial dive-site profiles.
- Marine-life observations with dates and attribution.
- Conditions presented as historical or typical, never guaranteed.
- Photography and conservation notes.
- Clear prompts to verify conditions, level, safety, and access with a qualified local operator.

**Boundary:** No operator rankings, live conditions, equipment advice, medical guidance, or claim
that the guide replaces professional briefing and supervision. Specialist content should be
reviewed by an appropriately qualified local dive professional before publication.

### Scooter Guide

**Role:** A field companion for experienced, appropriately licensed riders exploring from Joshua's
Point.

**Editorial territory:** Roads as journeys: surface, elevation, light, fuel, weather exposure,
parking, stopping points, and reasons not to continue.

**Core contents:**

- Rider and vehicle assumptions.
- Route families and route-character summaries.
- Approximate moving time and realistic day length.
- Road quality, fuel, parking, signal, weather, and turnaround notes.
- Offline overview maps.
- Future GPX files only with versioning, disclaimers, and a correction process.

**Boundary:** It is not navigation, rider training, vehicle hire, insurance advice, or a guarantee
that a route remains passable. Safety-critical facts remain public.

### Food Guide

**Role:** A guide to eating with curiosity and context rather than ranking restaurants.

**Editorial territory:** Markets, producers, coffee, bakeries, small restaurants, ingredients,
everyday dishes, and the people and seasons behind them.

**Core contents:**

- Regional ingredients and food culture.
- Considered profiles of places and people with consent.
- Market rhythms and practical visiting notes.
- Short food journeys that connect landscape and cooking.
- Dietary and allergen limitations stated honestly.

**Boundary:** No paid ranking, anonymous scoring, guaranteed hours, or unverified allergen claims.
Commercial relationships and complimentary hospitality must be disclosed.

### Editions and bundles

- Every file carries a product name, edition number, publication date, last reviewed date, and
  canonical support URL.
- Use semantic versions for operational clarity: major new edition `2.0`, material content update
  `1.1`, correction-only release `1.0.1`.
- A collection bundle may follow once at least two individual guides are independently useful.
- A bundle should be a purchasing convenience, not a fifth content product.

## 5. Pricing strategy

Pricing should fund editorial quality and maintenance, remain legible to both Philippine and
international customers, and avoid constant promotion.

### Pricing principles

1. Price the quality of research, editing, verification, design, and updates—not the page count.
2. Show the final tax-inclusive amount where the provider and jurisdiction allow it.
3. Avoid prices below US$10 while several candidate providers apply a US$0.50 fixed fee or require
   custom pricing for low-priced products.
4. Maintain deliberate PHP and international price books; do not imply that one is a live currency
   conversion of the other.
5. State the included update period before purchase. Never use ambiguous “lifetime updates.”
6. Keep discounts infrequent, simple, and available without countdown timers.
7. Do not sell paid placements or let commercial partners influence inclusion.

### Initial price-testing bands

These are research bands, not approved launch prices.

| Product | Philippine price test | International price test | Reasoning |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Explorer Guide | PHP 700–950 | US$13–17 | Broadest audience and first product |
| Dive Guide | PHP 900–1,300 | US$16–23 | Specialist review and narrower audience |
| Scooter Guide | PHP 900–1,300 | US$16–23 | Route verification and higher maintenance |
| Food Guide | PHP 700–950 | US$13–17 | Broad interest with recurring fact review |
| Future collection | 25–35% below the sum | 25–35% below the sum | Rewards buying the set without devaluing individual guides |

The two price books should be reviewed against actual purchasing power, provider settlement costs,
and tax display before launch. They should not silently drift with exchange rates.

### Validation before final pricing

- Interview 8–12 past or prospective guests about value and expected format.
- Test two price points with the same product and traffic quality; never change both copy and price
  in the same test.
- Measure completed purchase rate, net revenue after all fees and tax, refund rate, support time,
  and guide usage—not clicks alone.
- Ask buyers whether the guide saved planning time and improved confidence.
- Review whether complimentary guest access increases stay value without confusing the public
  price.
- Reassess price after the first substantial update, not after every small correction.

## 6. Sales page structure

The sales page should read like the opening pages of the publication. It should explain the object
calmly and let the reader decide without pressure.

1. **Editorial hero** — Product name, one-sentence promise, cover or real spread, edition date, and
   price. One clear purchase action.
2. **Why it exists** — A short note about helping people explore slowly and independently.
3. **What is inside** — A restrained contents overview organized by chapters or journeys.
4. **A look inside** — Several representative, readable spreads. Do not show only decorative cover
   mockups.
5. **Who it is for** — Clear reader and experience assumptions, including who should not rely on it.
6. **Choose a guide** — Explorer, Dive, Scooter, or Food, each with scope, format, edition, and
   price. Avoid ecommerce-style comparison clutter.
7. **Practical details** — File formats, approximate size, offline use, language, accessibility,
   delivery, update entitlement, and device requirements.
8. **Editorial trust** — Research method, last-reviewed date, contributors, corrections link, and
   limits of time-sensitive facts.
9. **License and refund summary** — Plain-language terms before checkout with links to full terms.
10. **FAQ** — The questions in Section 7, adapted to the released product.
11. **Final purchase opportunity** — Repeat only the product, price, format, and purchase action.

### Sales-page rules

- Use real guide pages and commissioned photography when available.
- Do not use fabricated testimonials, download counts, scarcity, crossed-out reference prices, or
  automatic timers.
- Label previews as samples and ensure they are accessible.
- Show currency and tax treatment before the customer leaves for hosted checkout.
- If checkout is hosted by a Merchant of Record, name the legal seller before purchase.
- Keep one canonical sales page per product and edition; use redirects when URLs change.

## 7. FAQ

The initial FAQ should answer these questions in plain language.

### What will I receive?

The current edition of the purchased guide in the formats listed on its sales page. At launch this
is expected to be an accessible PDF optimized for screens, with a print-ready PDF only where it
adds value. The exact files and sizes must be shown before checkout.

### Is this a printed book?

No. The launch product is digital. Customers may print a personal copy if the product license says
so. A physical edition would be a separate future product.

### Can I use it offline?

Yes, after the file has been downloaded to the device. External links, live maps, and provider
directions still require a connection.

### Does the guide replace maps, local advice, or a dive briefing?

No. Conditions, access, roads, weather, and services can change. Readers must verify current
conditions and use official notices, local advice, appropriate navigation, and qualified dive
professionals where relevant.

### How current is the information?

Every edition shows its publication and last-reviewed dates. Time-sensitive entries should also
show their own reviewed date where useful. Material corrections are published through the support
page and sent to eligible purchasers.

### Are updates included?

The sales page states the update entitlement for that edition. The proposed launch policy includes
corrections and minor updates for 12 months; a substantially expanded new edition may be sold
separately with an existing-customer offer.

### Can I share the guide?

The proposed personal license allows the buyer to use the guide with their accompanying travel
party and print one personal working copy. It does not allow forwarding the file, public upload,
resale, or commercial redistribution.

### Are routes guaranteed to be open or safe?

No. A route description records conditions observed or verified at a stated time. The reader is
responsible for current checks, legal compliance, appropriate experience, weather judgment, and
turning back.

### Does Joshua's Point receive payment for including places?

Inclusion is editorial. Any commercial relationship, complimentary visit, sponsorship, or affiliate
link must be disclosed beside the relevant content. Paid placement must not determine editorial
selection or ranking.

### What if I cannot download or open the file?

Contact the support address shown on the receipt. Access should be restored or an alternative
accessible format provided when reasonably possible.

### Can I request a refund?

Yes, in the circumstances and time period described in Section 13 and the terms shown before
checkout. Statutory consumer rights always take precedence.

### Is the guide available in other languages?

The launch language should be stated on the sales page. Do not imply translated availability until
each edition has been professionally translated and reviewed.

## 8. Checkout flow

The first checkout should be short, hosted where practical, and usable without creating an account.

### Customer flow

1. The customer reads the sales page and chooses one guide or an approved bundle.
2. The site passes only the product identifier, approved price/currency, and attribution data needed
   for the transaction to the provider.
3. Before payment, checkout shows product, edition, currency, tax, total, legal seller, email,
   refund summary, and links to terms and privacy information.
4. The customer chooses an available payment method and completes any required authentication.
5. The provider confirms payment server-to-server. A browser redirect alone must never grant
   access.
6. The customer sees a quiet confirmation page with the purchase status, delivery expectation,
   support contact, and a secure access action when available.
7. A transactional email provides the receipt and authenticated download or library link. Do not
   attach the guide to email.
8. The order record retains provider order ID, product and edition IDs, amount, currency, tax,
   entitlement, refund status, and delivery status. Do not store raw payment credentials.
9. A resend-access workflow uses the purchaser's verified email and does not expose whether another
   address has purchased.

### Consent and privacy

- Transactional receipts and material product notices are part of purchase fulfilment.
- Marketing email requires a separate, unticked consent where required.
- The guide should not require a customer to accept destination marketing to receive updates.
- Retain only the minimum customer and transaction data required for fulfilment, accounting,
  support, fraud handling, and legal obligations.
- Document which party is controller, processor, and legal seller for each shortlisted provider.

### Failure states

| State | Customer experience | Operational response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Payment pending | Clear pending message; no duplicate-payment prompt | Reconcile provider event and send delivery after confirmation |
| Payment failed | Neutral explanation and safe retry | Preserve cart context without storing card data |
| Paid, email missing | On-page access if secure plus resend option | Log delivery and support event |
| Paid, file unavailable | Apology and support route | Restore access or provide alternate delivery promptly |
| Duplicate purchase | Make support easy to reach | Refund the duplicate after verification |
| Refunded or disputed | Explain entitlement status | Revoke future access where lawful; preserve accounting record |

### Launch restraint

Do not build accounts, saved cards, upsells, order bumps, subscriptions, affiliates, coupons, or
multiple gateways into the first release unless a demonstrated customer or operational need
requires them.

### Important model distinction

A payment service provider processes payment while Joshua's Point generally remains responsible as
the seller for indirect-tax registration, calculation, collection, filing, refunds, disputes, and
delivery. A **Merchant of Record (MoR)** becomes the legal seller for the transaction and takes on
much of the payment and indirect-tax obligation. MoR status does not remove Joshua's Point's own
income-tax, accounting, consumer-support, content, or licensing responsibilities.

## 10. Digital delivery

### Launch package

Each purchase should resolve to a versioned package such as:

```text
southern-negros-explorer-guide-v1.0-screen.pdf
southern-negros-explorer-guide-v1.0-print.pdf   (only if offered)
read-me-and-corrections.html or .pdf
```

Do not add a print file by default if the screen edition prints well. Multiple files should solve a
real use case rather than create uncertainty.

### File standards

- Tagged PDF with logical reading order, bookmarks, meaningful headings, selectable text, language
  metadata, descriptive links, alt text, and sufficient contrast.
- Mobile-readable page size and body text; do not make pinch-zoom the only way to read.
- Compressed photography balanced against print and screen quality.
- Visible product, edition, publication date, last-reviewed date, copyright, license, support URL,
  and corrections URL.
- Map content accompanied by text orientation; color must not carry meaning alone.
- No third-party photography, maps, logos, or writing without documented rights for paid digital
  distribution.

### Access and security

- Deliver an authenticated or order-linked URL, not a public storage path.
- Do not attach large files to email.
- Allow reasonable re-downloads and a human recovery path. Security should not punish a traveller
  who changes phones.
- Avoid intrusive personalized watermarking at launch. If misuse becomes material, evaluate a quiet
  purchaser identifier with privacy disclosure before adding DRM.
- Keep a provider-independent entitlement export containing order, product, edition, purchaser,
  status, and update eligibility so migration remains possible.
- Maintain a tested backup of every released edition and its source assets.

### Operational release checklist

1. Editorial and factual review complete.
2. Rights and contributor credits verified.
3. Accessibility review and assistive-technology spot check complete.
4. Links, internal navigation, file metadata, and print test complete.
5. Version and update entitlement recorded.
6. Product file uploaded without deleting access to an edition still owed to buyers.
7. Test purchase, receipt, download, resend, refund, and export completed.
8. Corrections page and support response ready before sale opens.

## 11. Customer updates

### Update classes

| Class | Example | Customer treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Safety or material correction | Closure, prohibited access, dangerous route error | Correct public source immediately; notify affected buyers regardless of marketing consent where legally permitted as a product-critical notice |
| Patch `1.0.1` | Typo, broken link, minor factual correction | Replace current file, update changelog, optional concise product notice |
| Minor `1.1` | Reviewed entries, new photography, revised route notes | Included during the stated update window; notify eligible buyers |
| Major `2.0` | Substantially expanded or restructured new edition | New product or paid upgrade; offer clear existing-customer terms |

### Proposed launch entitlement

- Material corrections to the purchased edition remain available.
- Minor updates are included for 12 months from purchase.
- A major edition is not promised as free; eligible owners may receive a simple upgrade price.
- Never promise “lifetime updates,” because neither product lifetime nor vendor access is precise.

### Communication rules

- Separate transactional/product-critical messages from editorial newsletters and promotions.
- Every update message states what changed, the new version, reviewed date, and how to obtain it.
- Maintain a public corrections and version-history page even when the file is sold by a third party.
- Provide an unsubscribe for non-essential communication without removing receipt or purchase access.
- Keep customer email and entitlement exports portable in case the commerce provider changes.

## 12. Licensing

The final license must be reviewed for the legal entity and customer jurisdictions before launch.
The proposed product policy is:

### Personal licence

The buyer receives a non-exclusive, non-transferable right to:

- Download and use the purchased guide on their personal devices.
- Use it while travelling with their accompanying household or travel party.
- Print one personal working copy when printing is allowed on the sales page.

The buyer may not:

- Forward, upload, publicly share, resell, sublicense, or distribute the files.
- Sell printed copies or include the guide in a commercial package, rental, tour, class, or client
  deliverable.
- Extract and republish writing, photography, maps, route data, or datasets beyond lawful quotation
  and other applicable exceptions.
- Remove copyright, credit, license, version, or safety notices.

### Separate uses

- Joshua's Point guest copies may use a distinct property/guest entitlement rather than pretending
  one retail purchase covers all future guests.
- Tour operators, schools, accommodation partners, press, and commercial collaborators require a
  written licence.
- Contributor agreements must explicitly cover paid downloads, updates, marketing previews,
  territories, duration, attribution, and later editions.

### Intellectual-property safeguards

- Keep a rights register for every photograph, map layer, illustration, quotation, and contributed
  passage.
- Prefer provider-neutral original maps or correctly licensed data; a map screenshot is not
  automatically licensed for resale in a PDF.
- Do not imply ownership or endorsement of named destinations, communities, protected areas, or
  local businesses.

## 13. Refund policy

This is a product-policy proposal, not final legal text. It must be reconciled with Philippine law,
the buyer's mandatory consumer rights, and the selected provider's Merchant of Record or processor
terms.

### Proposed plain-language policy

Customers may contact support within 14 days of purchase. Joshua's Point should provide a refund or
prompt remedy for:

- Duplicate or clearly accidental duplicate purchases.
- A file that cannot be delivered, opened, or used as described after reasonable support.
- A materially defective, incomplete, or incorrectly supplied product.
- A materially misleading sales-page description.
- An accessibility barrier that prevents reasonable use and cannot be remedied with an alternative
  accessible file.
- Any circumstance where applicable law requires a refund or cancellation right.

For a correctly delivered digital guide that has been substantially accessed or downloaded, a
change of mind may be ineligible where law permits and where the customer gave any legally required
prior acknowledgement. Do not use a blanket “no refunds” statement.

### Policy rules

- Show a concise refund summary before payment and link the full policy.
- Tell the customer when instant digital access affects cancellation rights, if applicable.
- Keep the process human: order email, reason, and enough information to locate the transaction.
- Aim to acknowledge requests within two business days and communicate the expected provider
  processing time.
- Restore access when a technical issue can be solved; never make support an obstacle to a valid
  refund.
- Record reason categories to improve the product, not to challenge customers automatically.
- Refund tax, revoke future entitlement, and reconcile revenue according to provider rules.
- Publish one consistent policy across the sales page, checkout, receipt, and support page.

Before launch, obtain professional advice on digital-content cancellation consent, tax invoices,
privacy, and consumer remedies in the Philippines and priority buyer markets.

## 14. Launch strategy

Launch one excellent guide before creating a catalogue. The Explorer Guide is the appropriate
validation product because it uses the existing Destination System and serves the broadest audience;
this is a recommended sequence, not approval to begin implementation.

### Phase 0 — Editorial readiness

- Name an editor and fact owner for each chapter.
- Define the first edition's narrow destination list.
- Establish contributor agreements, fact sources, reviewed dates, and correction intake.
- Decide the actual legal seller and complete accounting/legal review.
- Create several representative pages before finalizing price or platform.

### Phase 1 — Product validation

- Interview 8–12 past guests and independent travellers.
- Test readable sample spreads on phone, tablet, laptop, and home printer.
- Validate price bands and update expectations.
- Run the payment-provider gate and trial in Section 9 without building a custom checkout.
- Write the support, privacy, licensing, and refund language.

### Phase 2 — Private field edition

- Release a clearly labeled pre-publication edition to a small group of guests and local reviewers.
- Ask them to use it during real planning and travel, not only review screenshots.
- Record errors, missing context, download friction, accessibility issues, and support questions.
- Pay or credit local specialist reviewers appropriately.
- Do not collect public reviews until the product is genuinely ready.

### Phase 3 — Quiet public launch

- Publish one canonical sales page and a generous preview.
- Announce first to past guests and the existing editorial audience with one calm email.
- Make the edition date, scope, limitations, format, price, and update policy explicit.
- Keep launch pricing simple; if used, one time-bounded introductory price without countdowns or
  false scarcity is enough.
- Monitor fulfilment and support closely for the first 72 hours.

### Phase 4 — Learn and maintain

- Publish corrections quickly and release a planned `1.1` after meaningful field feedback.
- Review net revenue against research, design, update, provider, support, tax, and contributor cost.
- Build the Dive, Scooter, or Food Guide next only when source depth and specialist review justify
  it—not because four products were named in advance.

### Launch measures

| Area | Measure |
| --- | --- |
| Trust | Correction volume and time to publish material corrections |
| Utility | Buyer-reported planning time saved and confidence gained |
| Delivery | Successful access rate, resend rate, time to resolve access issues |
| Commercial | Net revenue per order after tax and all provider/payout costs |
| Product quality | Refund reasons, support themes, accessibility issues, factual review age |
| Guest relationship | Guide inclusion or purchase rate among guests without pressure tactics |

Do not optimize for page views, email volume, or conversion at the expense of accuracy, regional
care, or the quiet Joshua's Point experience.

## 15. Future memberships

A membership should be introduced only when Joshua's Point has demonstrated a reliable editorial
cadence and customers repeatedly ask for continuing access. Subscription technology should not
create the obligation to publish before the editorial practice can sustain it.

### Possible future model

**Southern Negros Field Notes** could be an annual membership containing:

- Current editions of the guides included in the stated plan.
- Seasonal field notes and a maintained corrections archive.
- A small number of newly researched journeys each year.
- Existing-member pricing for major print editions or special publications.
- Optional early access to new editorial work, without manufacturing exclusivity.

### Membership rules

- Essential safety, closure, and correction information remains public.
- Promise a clear annual deliverable and update cadence, not vague “ongoing access.”
- One annual plan is preferable to multiple confusing tiers at first.
- Customers keep the editions downloaded during their paid term; access to future editions ends
  when the term ends.
- Renewal reminders, cancellation, receipts, failed-payment handling, and data export must be clear.
- Do not create a private community, discounts, or member-only events until there is a real owner
  and purpose for each.
- Grandfathering and price changes must be communicated well before renewal.

### Evidence required before membership

- At least two maintained guide editions.
- A year-long editorial calendar with named owners and budget.
- Demonstrated repeat purchase or update demand.
- Sustainable customer-support and correction response.
- Provider performance proven for one-time products and separately evaluated for recurring fees,
  tax, failed payments, cancellations, and exports.
